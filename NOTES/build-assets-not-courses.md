# Build Assets, Not Courses

Many people ask:

> How do I make money from my course?

I think that is the wrong question.

A better question is:

> How do I recover the investment I made in creating it?

I don't view a course as a collection of videos. I view it as an asset. Like any
business asset, it requires an upfront investment before it begins to produce
returns.

## Understand Your Real Investment

The investment is not merely money.

It includes:

- Time spent writing lessons.
- Hosting and domain costs.
- Design, recording, and editing expenses.
- Opportunity cost—the things I could have done instead.

Suppose I spend 500 hours creating a course.

If I value my time at KSh. 500 per hour, and incur KSh. 10,000 in additional
expenses, then my total investment becomes:

```text
(500 × KSh. 500) + KSh. 10,000 = KSh. 260,000
```

That means I don't need thousands of students to break even.

I simply need:

- 26 students paying KSh. 10,000 each.
- Or 52 students paying KSh. 5,000 each.
- Or 9 students paying KSh. 30,000 each.

Break-even is simply:

```text
Total Revenue ≥ Total Investment
```

## Don't Depend Solely on Course Sales

A course can create many streams of income.

It can lead to:

1. Premium course access.
2. Live cohorts and workshops.
3. Mentorship sessions.
4. Corporate training.
5. YouTube content that attracts students.
6. Freelance opportunities.
7. Paid code reviews.
8. Books and notes derived from the lessons.

The course itself is only one manifestation of the asset.

## Ask Better Questions

Instead of asking:

> How many people will buy my course?

I should ask:

> How many people do I need to help before I recover my investment?

That question changes everything.

It shifts my focus from selling to serving.

## Think Like a Product Engineer

I don't want to think like someone selling videos.

I want to think like someone building assets.

A well-crafted course is an asset that can continue paying dividends long after
the hard work has been completed.

Build once.

Improve continuously.

Help people.

Recover the investment.

Then let the asset compound.
